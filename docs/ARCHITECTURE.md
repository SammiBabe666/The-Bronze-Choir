# Architecture

The Bronze Choir is one Opus 2 synth engine, `bronzechoir`, inside the plugin `noladrift.bronzechoir`. It is plain C in fixed point. It uses no floating point, allocates nothing, and uses no C library. These are the source files and generated tables:

| File | What it is | Runs where |
|---|---|---|
| `bc_dsp.c`, `bc_dsp.h` | the sound: voices, modes, envelopes, sympathy, limiter | the audio path |
| `bc_tables.h` | the four bells' modal tables plus sine, exp2 and log2 tables (generated by `tools/make_tables.py`) | read-only data |
| `bc_page.c`, `bc_page.h` | T1 (the bells) and T2 (the modes) | the screen task only |
| `bronzechoir.c` | glue: the `opus_engine` struct, the shift layer, presets, encoder pushes | OS callbacks |
| `bc_presets.h` | the eight presets | data |
| `bc_fw.c`, `bc_fw.h` | the engine's only two firmware calls (`fw_shift_held`, `fw_snprintf`), the only file that includes `op1fw.h` | the screen task and OS callbacks |

## Signal flow

```
note on ──► voice (one of the OS's 6)
              │  the struck pitch picks the bell: <C3 cathedral, C3–B3 church, C4–B4 ceremonial, ≥C5 chime
              ▼
     strike: each mode's starting level = table level + strike tilt + metal tilt + size + loudness match
              │  + impact noise burst (coloured by strike, shorter for bigger bells)
              ▼
     render (128 samples @ 88.2 kHz, every block)
       ├─ per mode: sine oscillator, amplitude ramped linearly from last block's to this block's
       │    level (log domain, one exp2 lookup per mode per block)
       ├─ summed at 22.05 kHz (top mode < 5.5 kHz) or 44.1 kHz, then interpolated to 88.2 kHz
       └─ its bell's "send" (for the other bells' sympathy)
              ▼
     the OS sums the 6 voices and decimates to 44.1 kHz
              ▼
     block (64 samples @ 44.1 kHz): + sympathetic resonators, then a soft limiter above 0.7 FS
```

## The modes

Each bell is a fixed table of modes (`tools/make_tables.py`). Each mode has its frequency ratio to the played pitch, its level, a decay bias, a size coefficient and an optional doublet flag.

- **Cathedral, 13 modes:** a minor-third church bell's partials. Hum 0.5, prime 1.0, tierce 1.188, quint 1.506, nominal 2.0, deciem 2.514, superquint 2.662, octave nominal 3.011, then 4.166 and 5.433. The nominal, hum and tierce are doublets.
- **Church, 11 modes:** a sharper tierce (1.204), a flat quint (1.47), a shorter hum, and brighter upper partials.
- **Ceremonial, 10 modes:** a cast bell's voice: a strong fundamental and twelfth (3.0), 2.42, 2.08, 4.13, 5.38, 6.8, and a faint hum.
- **Chime, 8 modes:** sparse and widely spaced at 1.0, 2.32, 2.98, 4.25, 6.63 and 9.38.

The **played pitch is always the prime** (1.0), so the instrument plays in tune. The tables are ordered by importance. When voices pile up, each voice keeps the first modes of its table.

**Doublets** are the second half of a split mode. They sit at the same ratio and are detuned by the detune knob (up to 7 cents), so the two halves beat as a slightly asymmetric real bell's do. Detune also adds a small fixed mistuning between modes and a slow per-mode frequency wobble.

## Envelopes in the log domain

A mode's level is stored as log2 of its amplitude (Q20). An exponential decay is then just a constant subtracted once per block. Everything that shapes a mode's loudness (strike, metal, size, attack, loudness match) is a sum of logs. Once per block that sum becomes an amplitude through a 257-entry exp2 table, and it is ramped linearly over the block's samples. So no knob move, preset change or voice steal can produce a step in amplitude.

Each mode's T60 comes from the decay knob plus the mode's own decay bias, minus a frequency term. The result is **frequency-dependent damping**: higher partials and higher notes die sooner, and the hum rings longest. Releasing a key damps the bell by an amount the decay knob sets. Tails are capped (six octaves of level while held, 4.5 after release) so that every voice eventually finishes. The engine owns its envelope (`OPUS_ENGINE_OWN_ENVELOPE`) and tells the OS when a voice is silent (`finished`).

The **strike** starts each mode with a spectral tilt. A soft strike rolls off the upper modes and a hard one lifts them. It also sets a contact-time attack (0 to about 12 ms) and adds a short filtered noise burst for the impact.

## CPU strategy

The budget is 580,525 cycles per 64-sample block for everything the OP-1 does. Measured costs are in [TESTING.md](TESTING.md).

- **Reduced summing rate.** Modes are summed at 22.05 kHz when a voice's top mode is below 5.5 kHz, and at 44.1 kHz otherwise. The sum is then linearly interpolated to the 88.2 kHz the OS asks for. A mode at 44.1 kHz costs two budget units and one at 22.05 kHz costs one.
- **A shared mode budget.** Voices that are sounding share 54 units. One voice alone keeps every mode of its bell. Six voices each keep their most important modes, and never fewer than 4. The budget is worked out once per block, on the block's first render, from the number of live voices.
- **Inner loop.** 16×16-bit multiplies, a 1024-entry sine table in L1, two samples per loop iteration, and a scratch buffer in L1 (`opus_fast`).
- **Per-mode caches.** Decay step, metal gain and phase step are recomputed only when the knob, pitch or rate they depend on changes.
- **Silent modes are skipped** once they fall below −84 dB, as are modes above 19 kHz.
- **Amplitude hold.** Each voice's sum of mode amplitudes is held at or below 0.85 FS, so a single voice can never clip.

## Sympathetic resonance

Each bell has two Chamberlin state-variable band-pass resonators, tuned to that bell's last struck prime and its nominal (2×). They run at 22.05 kHz in `bc_block`.

- **No feedback.** A bell's resonators are fed **only by the other three bells' sends**. The resonators' output goes only to the final mix. It never reaches any resonator's input or any voice. A stable filter on a bounded input stays bounded, and the states are clamped as well.
- **Gain.** The knob follows a square law. The resonators' Q (60 to 360) follows the decay knob.
- **Cost.** Below a small gain the resonators are switched off and cost nothing. They are also skipped while their input and state are quiet.

The soft limiter after them leaves everything below 0.7 FS untouched and eases peaks above it towards full scale.

## The pages, kept apart from the audio

`bc_page.c` runs only in the screen task. It **reads** single-word values that the audio publishes: per-bell strike counters and strike strength (`bc_strikes`, `bc_strike_amt`), ring levels (`bc_ring`), sympathy levels (`bc_sym`) and the knobs as heard. It **never writes anything the audio reads**, so the animation cannot disturb the sound and costs the audio nothing.

**T1** shows four bells in line art, drawn in the four encoder colours only: blue frame, green hangers and yokes, white bells and clappers, orange strike lights.

- The bells sit at four sizes and heights. From left to right: cathedral, church, ceremonial, chime.
- Each bell is a damped pendulum with its own period: 1.7 s, 1.4 s, 1.1 s and 0.85 s.
- A new strike in a bell's register pushes it, by an amount set by the strike strength.
- Its clapper hangs on a second, lagging pendulum that stops at the bell's rim.
- An orange light flashes at the strike point for 7 frames.
- With sympathy on, ringing bells nudge the others slightly.
- The frame and labels are drawn once and cached by the core (`opus_page_still`). Only the bells and bars are redrawn each frame, using the cheap stroke line.
- Under the bells are the four knob bars plus thin bars for sympathy and detune. With shift held, the green and orange labels change to sympathy and detune. The preset name shows with `*` once it has been edited.

**T2** shows the newest voice's modes: one line per mode at its log-frequency ratio, as tall as that mode is ringing. Doublets are drawn in orange.

## Memory

- **L1 (`opus_fast`):** the sine table and the summing scratch buffer, about 2.5 KB.
- **Voice state:** about 450 bytes per voice × 6.
- **Code and data:** the `.opmod` is 21 KB. The image is 16.5 KB plus 1.9 KB zero-fill.

## The host and simulator harnesses

- **`host/`** compiles the **same** `bc_dsp.c`, `bc_page.c` and `bronzechoir.c` for a computer. It links them against `os_sim.c`, a stand-in for the OS and Opus core: six voices with stealing, decimation, the knobs, the shift layer, encoder turns and pushes, and drawing calls recorded as text. That harness drives the stress tests, the preset analysis, the screenshots and the video.
- **`sim/`** compiles `bc_dsp.c` for the Blackfin and counts cycles in GDB's Blackfin instruction simulator.
